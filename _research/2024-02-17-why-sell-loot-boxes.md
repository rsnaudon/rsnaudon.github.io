  **Abstract:** A monopolist sells an indivisible good to risk-averse consumers with unit demand who share a common valuation but differ in wealth. By selling a lottery that delivers the good stochastically, the firm effectively makes the good divisible. When consumers can purchase the lottery repeatedly, they optimally choose their number of attempts and hence their overall probability of receiving the good. Richer consumers choose more attempts, so the repeated lottery screens consumers by wealth and can generate more revenue than does a posted price. This repeated lottery is nevertheless dominated by a menu of one-shot lotteries. We show that selling bundles of a virtual currency that consumers then spend on lotteries closes the gap, rationalizing why this selling scheme is prevalent in video games.
