  **Abstract:** People often keep quiet when they expect their views to be unpopular. I study a dynamic model in which agents with private positions on a controversial topic choose between voicing an opinion and staying silent. Speakers balance their true views against conformity to a social consensus, which they learn about only through a noisy public signal of past opinions. Uncertainty about the consensus itself deters expression: when it is large enough, no one speaks. Because the signal aggregates only voiced opinions, the learned consensus is biased toward those willing to speak, giving rise to silent majorities whose views are underrepresented in public discourse.
