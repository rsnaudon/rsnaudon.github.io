  **Abstract:** People who fear their views are unpopular may choose to stay silent. I study a dynamic model in which agents with privately known positions on a controversial topic decide each period whether to voice an opinion or stay silent. Speaking trades off expressing one's true view against conforming to a social consensus that no one observes directly. Agents learn about that consensus only through a noisy public signal of previously expressed opinions. Uncertainty about the consensus itself deters expression: when it is large enough, no one speaks. Because the signal aggregates only voiced opinions, the consensus agents learn about is biased toward those willing to speak, giving rise to silent majorities whose views are underrepresented in public discourse.
